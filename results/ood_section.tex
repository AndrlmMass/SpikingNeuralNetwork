\subsection{Rejecting out-of-distribution input}
\label{sec:ood}

We asked whether the network also identifies input it was never trained on, holding an
MNIST-trained model fixed while presenting KMNIST, notMNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and SVHN.
The predictive entropy of Section~\ref{sec:sel_pred} separates novel from familiar input
poorly, at $\mathrm{AUROC}$ $0.694$--$0.870$ across the four probes, but that is a
property of the readout rather than of the representation. Two scores read from the
excitatory rates instead do considerably better: the Mahalanobis distance to the nearest
class-conditional Gaussian fitted on held-out MNIST reaches $0.847$--$0.968$, and the
concentration of activity across the ten class groups---the entropy of the group-pooled
rate distribution, which requires no fitted parameters at all---reaches
$0.765$--$0.907$.\footnote{The Mahalanobis score has fitted parameters, so its threshold
is calibrated on a held-out half of the MNIST calibration split; calibrating on the same
data it was fitted to makes in-distribution scores optimistic and inflates the realised
false-rejection rate from $2.5\%$ to $11.3\%$.} Averaged over the probes, moving from
predictive entropy to the Mahalanobis score raises $\mathrm{AUROC}$ from $0.775$ to
$0.896$ and lowers the false-positive rate at $95\%$ true-positive rate from $0.71$ to
$0.22$.

A detector computed on raw pixels and using no network at all still outperforms both: the
residual outside MNIST's top-$50$ principal subspace reaches $0.971$--$0.999$
(Figure~\ref{fig:ood}). We report this as a limitation rather than a defeat, because the
comparison is specific to the benchmark: these are centred $28 \times 28$ grayscale
images on a uniform background, so the in-distribution manifold is genuinely
low-dimensional in pixel space and a linear subspace model captures it almost exactly.
The network's contribution is visible where such low-level cues are weakest. On KMNIST,
the probe matched to MNIST in evoked firing rate ($0.00213$ against $0.00225$), mean image
intensity falls to $0.745$ while the Mahalanobis score holds $0.879$; on SVHN, colour
photographs that mean brightness alone almost separates ($0.980$), the ordering reverses.
At a threshold refusing $2.5\%$ of valid MNIST, the group-concentration score refuses
$27$--$76\%$ of out-of-distribution input and the Mahalanobis score $63$--$94\%$, against
$88$--$99\%$ for the pixel residual. All of these numbers rest on a single trained
network.
