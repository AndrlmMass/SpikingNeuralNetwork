\subsubsection{Rejecting out-of-distribution data}
\label{sec:res:ood}
We ask whether the network also identifies input it was never trained on, holding an
MNIST-trained model fixed while presenting KMNIST, notMNIST, and FMNIST. The predictive
entropy of Section~\ref{sec:sel_pred} separates novel from familiar input poorly, at
$\mathrm{AUROC}$ $0.773$--$0.884$ across the three datasets, but that is a property of the
readout rather than of the representation. Two scores extracted from the excitatory rates do
better on notMNIST and FMNIST, though neither improves on entropy for KMNIST. The Mahalanobis
distance to the nearest class-conditional Gaussian fitted on held-out MNIST reaches
$0.884$--$0.969$, and the concentration of activity across the ten class-specific
populations, the entropy of the rate distribution pooled per population, which requires no
fitted parameters at all, reaches $0.765$--$0.906$.\footnote{The Mahalanobis score has fitted
parameters, so its threshold is calibrated on a held-out half of the MNIST calibration split;
fitting and calibrating it on the same data makes in-distribution scores optimistic and
inflates the realized false-rejection rate from the nominal $2.5\%$ to $10.5\%$.} Averaged
over the datasets, moving from predictive entropy to the Mahalanobis score raises
$\mathrm{AUROC}$ from $0.818$ to $0.914$ and lowers the false-positive rate at $95\%$
true-positive rate from $0.66$ to $0.21$.

A detector computed on raw pixels and using no network at all still outperforms both, with
the residual outside MNIST's top-$50$ principal subspace reaching $0.983$--$0.999$
(Figure~\ref{fig:ood}). We report this as a limitation rather than a defeat, because the
comparison is specific to the benchmark. These are centered $28 \times 28$ grayscale images on
a uniform background, so the in-distribution manifold is genuinely low-dimensional in pixel
space and a linear subspace model captures it almost exactly. The network's contribution is
visible where such low-level cues are weakest. On KMNIST, the dataset matched to MNIST in
evoked firing rate ($0.00213$ against $0.00225$), mean image intensity falls to $0.745$ while
the Mahalanobis score holds $0.884$. At a threshold refusing $2.5\%$ of valid MNIST, the
group-concentration score refuses $28$--$71\%$ of out-of-distribution input and the
Mahalanobis score $63$--$94\%$, against $90$--$99\%$ for the pixel residual. Each number is
the mean over five independently trained networks, between which the two rate-based scores
vary by less than $0.01$ in $\mathrm{AUROC}$ and predictive entropy by up to $0.07$.
