\subsubsection{Protocol}
We define the contribution of each configuration $c$ relative to a supervised reference,
\textit{Plastic RF}, which combines receptive field connectivity, the R-Hebb rule in the
excitatory (hidden) layer, and the softmax delta rule in the readout. Each of the other two
configurations changes a single component. In \textit{Random}, the receptive field
connectivity is replaced by random weights of matched input drive, isolating the effect of
weight structure. In \textit{Frozen}, the receptive field weights are held at their
initialization (no R-Hebb), while the readout still learns with the softmax delta rule,
isolating the contribution of hidden-layer learning. In Equation~\eqref{eq:glmm_lin}, $c$
ranges over these three conditions with \textit{Plastic RF} as the reference, and we fit the
model separately to the learned readout and to the linear classifier.

\subsubsection{Receptive fields under reward-modulated plasticity}
Under R-Hebb, plasticity improves on the frozen receptive fields on every dataset, whereas the
benefit of receptive fields over random connectivity depends on the dataset
(Figure~\ref{fig:phase2_glmm}). The beta-binomial GLMM (Table~\ref{tab:glmm_phase2}) takes
\textit{Plastic RF} on MNIST as its reference, so each configuration coefficient is the
logit-scale effect of one change on MNIST, and each interaction is how that effect shifts on
another dataset.

Disabling R-Hebb has the larger effect of the two changes. On MNIST, \textit{Frozen} lowers
the learned readout's accuracy from $94.5\%$ to $85.4\%$ ($\hat{\beta}_2 = -1.085$,
$p<.001$), and although its interactions with the other datasets are positive, none outweighs
this main effect, so \textit{Frozen} is the least accurate configuration on every dataset. On
KMNIST the interaction does not differ from zero ($\hat{\beta}_9 = 0.095$, $p = .183$), which
leaves the largest gap of all, $53.4\%$ against $75.5\%$.

Replacing the receptive fields with random connectivity also lowers accuracy on MNIST, to
$90.2\%$ ($\hat{\beta}_1 = -0.640$, $p<.001$), but all three of its interactions are positive
($p<.001$). On KMNIST the interaction ($\hat{\beta}_8 = 0.457$) recovers only part of the main
effect, so the receptive fields keep their lead, whereas on FMNIST and notMNIST it exceeds
the main effect ($\hat{\beta}_6 = 0.847$ and $\hat{\beta}_{10} = 1.218$) and random
connectivity becomes the more accurate of the two, reaching $88.0\%$ on notMNIST against
$80.4\%$ for \textit{Plastic RF}. The linear classifier reproduces the sign of every
coefficient ($\hat{\beta}_1 = -0.737$ and $\hat{\beta}_2 = -0.665$, with all interactions
positive), so the dependence on the dataset is a property of the representation rather than
of the readout that decodes it. Seed-to-seed variation of the learned readout differs about
sixteenfold between datasets, from $\hat{\phi}_{\text{FMNIST}} = 320$ to
$\hat{\phi}_{\text{KMNIST}} = 5239$, so we estimate its dispersion separately for each
dataset, while the seed variance itself is negligible.

Taken together, directed plasticity supplies what the unsupervised model lacked. Once the
rule receives information about the task, it improves on the frozen receptive fields on every
dataset, where every unsupervised rule fell short of them. The receptive fields themselves,
however, help only on handwritten digits and characters and cost accuracy on garments and
letter glyphs, so their benefit depends on whether the data carry the local, oriented
structure they assume.
