% --- Abstract: replace from "On datasets the network never saw" to "rejects $88$--$99\%$." ---
On datasets the network never saw, however, entropy separates novel from familiar input
poorly (mean AUROC $0.82$), so we compare it against scores read from the network's
internal activity and from the raw pixels. The Mahalanobis distance in the excitatory
rate space raises the mean AUROC to $0.91$ and rejects $63$--$94\%$ of unseen input while
refusing only $2.5\%$ of valid digits, outperforming the parameter-free concentration of
activity across class-specific neuronal populations, yet a subspace model of the raw pixels,
which uses no network at all, rejects $90$--$99\%$.

The out-of-distribution experiment (Section~\ref{sec:res:ood}) instead trains its MNIST models
on $55{,}000$ images and holds out $5{,}000$ as the calibration set from which its rejection
thresholds are estimated, because thresholds set in the tail of the confidence distribution
need more held-out samples to be precise; it is tested on the same official $10{,}000$-image
split.

Read by the same linear classifier in both experiments, replacing the receptive fields with
random connectivity costs little on MNIST in the unsupervised model ($\hat{\beta}_1 = -0.072$)
and about ten times as much in the supervised one ($\hat{\beta}_1 = -0.737$). Through its own
readout the supervised network reaches $94.5\%$ on MNIST and $75.5\%$ on KMNIST, the two
datasets on which the weight connectivity and the rule agree.

% --- Limitations: replace from "The supervised contrast also depends on which decoder" to

The supervised contrast also depends on which decoder reads the representation. The learned
readout and the linear classifier agree in the sign of every coefficient but differ in
magnitude, most clearly for \textit{Frozen} ($\hat{\beta}_2 = -1.085$ against $-0.665$ on
MNIST), so each statement of that contrast has to name its decoder.
